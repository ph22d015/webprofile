Research rarely moves in a straight line. A deposition run gives a film that is a little off, an alignment that worked yesterday drifts today, and a measurement raises a better question than the one it was meant to answer.

What keeps the work going is less about motivation and more about curiosity: wanting to know *why* the film came out that way.
A failed run is still data, and often the most informative kind.

Patience is the other half. Good samples, clean measurements and honest conclusions take time, and pretending otherwise usually costs more time later.

Outside the lab I read, cook and take photographs. All three reward the same habits: paying attention, trying again, and noticing small changes.
